Technical Architecture, Knowledge Recombination, and the Vertical Boundary of the Firm: Empirical Evidence from Cyber-Physical Innovation in the Automotive Industry

Te Lai (Duke Kunshan University)  •  October 2026

Study 1: Macro Paradigm Symbiosis

Target: PNAS / Nature Energy

Documents clean electric platforms as the physical conduit for embodied AI over ICEs. Deploys 3.2M electric powertrain, 5.8M ICE, and 510K autonomous driving patents with 48.9K 768-D PatentSBERTa embeddings to capture a -15.6% semantic distance collapse.

Study 2: Intra-Firm Architectural Reorganization

Target: Research Policy / Management Science

Formulates a within-firm natural experiment across 25 global automakers (152 platform generations, 264 models). Exploits the 2–10 year 'Conversion EV' retrofitting window to show modular substitution yields zero recombinant growth, whereas native platforms trigger persistent jumps.

Study 3: Inter-Firm Innovation Chains

Target: Strategic Management Journal / Research Policy

Examines OEM-supplier vertical boundaries under standardized vehicle motion control (VMC API). Using 111K patents from top-10 chassis mega-suppliers (Bosch, Continental, ZF) and dyadic citations, proves OEM upward intrusion and supplier commoditization.

Abstract: How do shifts in underlying technical architecture govern the micro-foundations of knowledge recombination and the vertical boundaries of the firm? Existing innovation economics largely attributes search frictions to cognitive distance or organizational boundaries, while the digital innovation literature widely presumes that layered modular architectures enable frictionless software-hardware decoupling. In cyber-physical systems, however, cognitive algorithms must execute through electromechanical actuators governed by microsecond-level deterministic feedback loops. Under these conditions, the physical interface architecture functions as the binding conduit for recombinant search.

We investigate this across three sequentially linked studies spanning 3.2M electric powertrain patents, 5.8M ICE patents, 510K verified autonomous driving patents, 152 platform generations across 25 automakers, and 111K Tier-1 supplier patents. We document sharp distributional collapse in 768-dimensional PatentSBERTa embeddings, exploit a 2- to 10-year within-firm modular retrofit ("Conversion EV") counterfactual window, and demonstrate how standardized abstraction layers induce OEM upward intrusion and supplier commoditization.

Global Carbon Pricing and Clean Energy Innovation

Working Paper (2026)
Jingbo Cui (Duke Kunshan University), Te Lai (Duke Kunshan University), and Yang Zheng (Aston Business School & LSE Grantham)
This paper provides a comprehensive causal evaluation of the impacts of carbon taxes versus Emissions Trading Schemes (ETS) on global clean energy innovation. To map over 5 million granted international patents (1990–2022) to more than 600 granular technologies in the International Energy Agency’s (IEA) Clean Energy Technology Guide, we designed a hybrid multi-model consensus pipeline integrating Large Language Models (DeepSeek, ChatGPT, Gemini). The methodology achieves an unprecedented 80% classification accuracy against expert spot-checks, outperforming standard IPC/CPC taxonomies (25%). Econometrically, we employ staggered Difference-in-Differences (DID) and Poisson Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood (PPML) regressions to isolate policy-induced technological transitions across technology readiness levels (TRLs).
Method: Staggered DID & PPML Data: 5M+ Global Patents LLM: DeepSeek / Gemini Consensus

The Evolution of The Network: The Evidence from China

Conference Proceeding / Under Review
Jingbo Cui (Duke Kunshan University) and Te Lai (Duke Kunshan University)  •  Presented at The 6th East Asian Association of Environmental and Resource Economics (AAERE) Conference, Seoul National University
Using a large panel of over 500,000 collaborative patent records and financial metrics across 5,000+ Chinese listed companies, this paper executes the first systematic longitudinal visualization and econometric study of national corporate patent networks in China. Using social network analysis in Python and fixed-effects panel regressions in Stata, we quantify the centrality of firms within the collaborative topology and demonstrate that network centrality confers a statistically significant 0.3% excess return and strongly accelerates green technology absorption.
